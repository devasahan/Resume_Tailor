METRICS: use figures sparingly so each one stands out. Put a measurable result in only about 2 of every 5 bullets: at most 2 bullets per employer (usually 1 for older or short roles), and never two figure bullets in a row. Every job's first bullet is always one of them, and it gives two figures, its size and its result (for example, instead of "Built backend services using Java, MySQL and AWS": "Built the matchmaking service for 83K concurrent players in Java on AWS, cutting queue-to-lobby time by 22% (from 41 to 32 seconds)"). Every other bullet shows its impact in words (what changed, who benefited, the scope). Use the candidate's real numbers from the hints first, exactly as given. Where none are given, write realistic, conservative estimates that fit the company, the seniority of the role and the length of the stay, and make them look like real measurements rather than round guesses: never use numbers ending in 0 or 5 (no 25%, 30%, 35%, 40%, 50+), never put "~", "approximately" or "about" before a figure, and give about a third of the figures one decimal place. A percentage means little on its own (20% of 5,000 or of 500,000?), so every percentage that describes a change ("by 22%", "21% faster", "a 9.4% drop") is followed by its base in brackets: the amount with its period, or the values before and after. Examples of the right style: "cut pipeline runtime by 23.4% (from 47 to 36 minutes)", "reduced cloud spend by 17.8% ($41,000 a year)", "cut weekly reporting time by 71.4% (from 7 hours to 2 hours)", "processing 1.7TB of daily events", "supporting 38 analysts". The base must agree with the percentage (from 41 to 32 is 22%); money may end in ,000 ($41,000, never $40,000). A percentage and its base count as one figure, and a level needs no base ("99.9% uptime", "from 61% to 87% test coverage"). Where the hints give a percentage without its base, keep it and add a realistic base. Vary the kind of metric (percentage, time, volume, count, money), never repeat the same figure, and use at most one figure in any other bullet. The candidate is shown every estimated figure and asked to confirm or replace it, so keep them believable rather than impressive.
