METRICS: use figures sparingly so each one stands out. Put a measurable result in only about 2 of every 5 bullets: at most 2 bullets per employer (usually 1 for older or short roles), and never two figure bullets in a row. Every job's first bullet is always one of them, and it gives two figures, its size and its result (for example, instead of "Built backend services using Java, MySQL and AWS": "Built the matchmaking service for 83K concurrent players in Java on AWS, cutting queue-to-lobby time by 22%"). Every other bullet shows its impact in words (what changed, who benefited, the scope). Use the candidate's real numbers from the hints first, exactly as given. Where none are given, write realistic, conservative estimates that fit the company, the seniority of the role and the length of the stay, and make them look like real measurements rather than round guesses: never use numbers ending in 0 or 5 (no 25%, 30%, 35%, 40%, 50+), never put "~", "approximately" or "about" before a figure, and give about a third of the figures one decimal place. Examples of the right style: "cut pipeline runtime by 23.4%", "reduced cloud spend by 17.8%", "processing 1.7TB of daily events", "supporting 38 analysts". Vary the kind of metric (percentage, time, volume, count), never repeat the same figure, and use at most one figure in any other bullet. The candidate is shown every estimated figure and asked to confirm or replace it, so keep them believable rather than impressive.
